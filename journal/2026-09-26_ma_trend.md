# ma_trend (2026-09-26)

- **Idea:** Hold the asset when its price is above its 200-day moving average, otherwise hold cash.
  Source: Meb Faber, "A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation" (2007), plus decades of trader folklore.
- **Data:** ⚠️ **SYNTHETIC demo data** (real downloads were blocked in the build environment). SPY and XIU.TO, 2005 – Sept 2026.
- **Parameters:** ma_length = 200, band = 0. Textbook values, not tuned.
- **Result (demo data):**

  | | SPY-like | XIU.TO-like |
  |---|---|---|
  | Sharpe, train → test | 0.26 → 0.60 | 0.36 → 0.67 |
  | Test Sharpe vs buy-and-hold / SPY | 0.60 vs 0.68 / 0.68 | 0.67 vs 0.33 / 0.68 |
  | Test CAGR vs buy-and-hold | 9.0% vs 13.3% | 8.2% vs 4.8% |
  | Max drawdown vs buy-and-hold | -51.9% vs -47.1% | -50.3% vs -51.9% |
  | Trades (total / test) | 82 / 26 | 85 / 24 |

- **Verdict:** **FAIL**.
  - SPY: "It failed 3 of 7 checks. Main problem (costs): after costs it does not beat simply buying and holding, so it isn't earning its complexity."
  - XIU.TO: "It failed 1 of 7 checks. Main problem (costs): after costs it does not beat simply buying and holding, so it isn't earning its complexity."
- **Lesson:** On the SPY-like series the trend filter got whipsawed in the V-shaped 2020 crash: it sold near the bottom
  and bought back higher, ending up with a *deeper* worst fall than just holding. On the XIU.TO-like series it beat its
  own buy-and-hold comfortably, but only tied the broad index (0.67 vs 0.68). The lab's rule says it must beat both, so
  "better than holding a weak market" isn't enough if simply holding the S&P was better. Since this is synthetic data,
  none of this says anything about the real rule. **Next step: re-run on real data.**
